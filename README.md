HLPR Ministries — The Master Ministry Base

A parameterized church-site engine for the free-mockup funnel — Lovable build spec

What this is: A build specification for a single reusable Lovable site base that powers every church mockup you produce. You build the base once. For each new church you swap a defined set of variables — brand, copy, imagery, one signature touch — and a custom-feeling homepage falls out in a few hours, not a few days. The structure and the conversion logic are permanent; only the skin and the soul change per church.

What this is not: A rigid template that stamps out identical sites. The whole design goal is that no two mockups read as the same template, even though they share a skeleton. A template is only a liability when it looks like one.

Who does what: This document is the spec. You build it in Lovable from the prompt in Part 6. Claude writes specs; Lovable builds the site.

The one rule that governs every word on the page: the methodology stays invisible. The conversion principles in Part 5 power how the page is built, but their names never appear in anything a church or a church visitor sees. No "above the fold," no "value equation," no framework names, no narrating why a section exists. Visitors feel a site that just works; they never see the machinery.

Part 1 — The strategic frame

The tension this solves

The free mockup in 72 hours is one of your strongest value props. But hand-building a genuinely unique site for every unpaid lead is bad business — it does not scale, and most mockups never convert to a paid build. So you need the free mockup to feel custom without costing you a custom build. Those are two different problems, and separating them is the whole solve.

Looking templated kills conversions. Being templated under the hood is exactly what makes the free offer survivable. The Master Base is templated structure with custom presentation. The visitor never sees the structure; they see their church, in their colors, with their words and their people.

The flywheel

Every mockup you build against the base also improves the base. A pattern you nail for one established church carries to the next. So your mockup speed compounds: the tenth is faster and sharper than the first, and the base gets better every time. That is the opposite of bespoke-every-time — it is an asset that appreciates.

Build the base now, against a real church. You have Mark / Odessa First as the forcing function and a locked ICP. The base you build for Mark is the base you reuse for Donald, Timbo, Fredia, and every cold-call mockup. Build the reusable thing while you have a real church to pressure-test it.

Who the homepage is actually for

A church homepage has two audiences; you build for the harder one:

The seeker / first-time visitor (primary). Deciding whether to walk into a room full of strangers. Dominant emotion is apprehension — will I be welcome, what do I wear, where do I park, what about my kids, will someone corner me. The homepage's #1 job is to dissolve that and make showing up feel safe and easy.

The returning member (secondary). Needs fast utility: times, events, give, sermons. Never make a member dig.

The conversion goal is not "buy now." It is "Plan Your Visit" / "I'm New." Every section either moves a nervous stranger one step closer to attending, or serves a member fast. If a section does neither, cut it.

Why homepage-only is the right teaser: the homepage carries 100% of the first impression and the entire "should I visit" decision. Fix only the homepage and you've shown the most important transformation — fast enough to give away, compelling enough to make them want the rest.

Selling line for the call: "This is your homepage, rebuilt to do the one thing your current site doesn't — turn a first-time visitor into someone who shows up Sunday. The full site builds out every page like this once we get going."

Part 3 — The Master Base: homepage section architecture

This is the skeleton you clone for every church, top to bottom. Free mockup = sections 1–6 + footer; the rest build out in the paid site (base contains all of them so the paid build is just as fast).

1. Sticky top bar (in free mockup)

What: Slim fixed header — logo left; nav right (New Here, About, Times & Location, Give); one accent "Plan Your Visit" button. Mobile collapses to logo + always-visible Plan Your Visit (not buried in hamburger).

Job: Primary action reachable from any scroll position, any device.

Varies: Logo, name, accent color, nav labels.

2. Hero (the make-or-break section) (in free mockup)

What: Full-width hero, real photo of THIS church's people/building (never stock). One clear headline naming who they are + the welcome, a one-line subhead, one prominent "Plan Your Visit" button. Service time + city visible here or just beneath.

Job: In ~5 seconds a stranger understands this is a church, who it's for, how to take the next step, and that they'd be welcome. Decides whether most visitors stay. Earns the majority of design effort.

Varies: Hero image, headline, mission line, service time, city, accent color, logo.

3. Service times + location + map (utility band) (in free mockup)

What: Clean band below hero — day/time(s), full address, embedded map, quick practical answers (parking, what to wear, service length). Big, scannable.

Job: When and where, answered before any scrolling effort. Highest-leverage friction removal; most churches fail it.

Varies: Times, address, map pin, practical Q&A.

4. "New here? Here's what to expect" (in free mockup)

What: Reassurance written to the nervous first-timer. 3–4 short warm blocks with icons: what a service feels like, how long, what to wear (come as you are), what happens with kids, that no one will single them out. Ends with Plan Your Visit.

Job: The single most important trust section on a church site — and almost no small church has it. Dissolves the specific fears that stop attendance.

Varies: Kids/childcare, dress norms, service length, tone.

5. Mission / story / who we are (in free mockup)

What: Short, human — mission in their words + a brief real story (origin, heart, beliefs), paired with a genuine photo. Short paragraphs, generous spacing.

Job: Connection and credibility. People attend churches whose heart they feel. Real story + real faces = "people I could belong with."

Varies: Mission, origin story, beliefs, photo, voice (often handed to you verbatim).

6. Light social proof / life of the church (in free mockup)

What: Warm, open grid of real moments — congregation, events, serving. Surface genuine testimonies or a real number/date naturally. Everything visible; no carousels.

Job: Shows the church is alive and full of real people. Visual proof of belonging beats any claim. Hidden content doesn't get seen.

Varies: Photos, real numbers/dates, testimonies. If proof is thin, lean on warm imagery — never a disclaimer about what's missing.

7. Ministries / groups (paid build only) — scannable cards (name, one warm line). Answers "is there a place for me/my family?"

8. Events / what's coming up (paid build only) — a few upcoming events; signals active community and gives a low-pressure on-ramp.

9. Sermons / messages (paid build only) — latest message featured + embed/link to where they already publish; lets a seeker sample teaching before committing.

10. Give (paid build only) — clean, trustworthy giving section/page with their existing method embedded; plain language.

11. Contact / plan your visit (conversion footer) (in free mockup)

What: Final band repeating the action — short form (name, email, "when are you thinking of coming?") or clear contact path, plus time, address, map, phone, socials. Page ends on the action.

Job: Captures the visitor who scrolled the whole page; gives members the info where they'll look last. Action present top, middle, end — three chances.

Varies: Form fields, contact details, socials, accent color.

Order is not negotiable. Welcome and the next step come before story; story before asks. A nervous stranger needs "I'm welcome and here's how to come" resolved before they care about your history, and they'll care about history before they'll give.

Part 4 — The variable / swap layer

Everything that makes a mockup feel custom lives here, and only here. The skeleton never changes; change these four things per church.

The four swap dimensions

Brand. Their colors (primary/secondary/neutral), logo, fonts — from their site or Facebook. Two fonts max (one display, one body). One accent color, used only for buttons/primary actions.

Copy / voice. Their actual mission, real times, real ministry names, in their voice. Reuse what they have. Traditional and contemporary churches should read differently. Lead every section with the benefit to the visitor, not a label.

Imagery. Their building, congregation, events (Facebook or supplied). Real faces beat everything. No obvious stock; no apology for what's missing.

One signature touch. Exactly one small custom element per church (building-as-hero, a logo motif, their values framework as section structure, a distinctive color treatment). One is enough to tip "template" into "made for us."

Part 5 — Conversion logic (INTERNAL reference only)

Hard boundary. This part exists so the base is built on sound conversion psychology. The named principles here are for you and Claude only. None of this language ever appears in a church site, mockup, proposal, or anything a client/visitor reads. If a sentence on a live page would reveal the mechanism, delete it.

The value-equation lens (why the order works)

Every section raises the perceived value of attending while lowering its perceived cost.

Lever For a church visitor Where the base delivers it Dream outcome Belonging, a spiritual home, a place for family Hero copy + imagery, mission/story, life grid Likelihood it's for me "People like me are here, I'd be welcome" Real faces, what-to-expect, light social proof Time / speed to value See when/where, take a step now Service band above scroll, persistent Plan-Your-Visit Effort & fear Easy and safe to show up first time What-to-expect (kids, dress, parking), one clear action

Premium = trust, not flash

"Premium" means it looks like the church invested care: organized, clean, consistent type/color/spacing, real imagery, logical flow. Cheap-feeling pages (wall-of-text, clutter, inconsistent branding, stock photos, dated layouts) erode trust. The base bakes the trust signals in so every mockup clears the bar by default.

The principles, mapped to where they live

Highest-leverage section gets the most care — the hero decides most visitors (§2).

Imagery does the selling — real, emotional photos of real people; no stock (§2, §5, §6).

Show proof, don't claim it — open, visible warmth; no carousels; no disclaimers (§6).

Reduce fear at the point of action — what-to-expect answers the exact hesitations where someone decides (§4).

Visual hierarchy guides the eye — size/weight/color flow headline → body → action (Part 4).

Reduce choice — one primary action; cut anything serving neither seeker nor member (whole page).

Speak the visitor's language; lead with benefit — warm, plain, human; every section leads with what the visitor gets (all copy).

Humanize — real story, faces, voice (§5).

Repeat the action — present top, middle, end (§2, §4, §11).

Part 6 — Lovable build prompt (scaffold the base)

Paste this into Lovable to scaffold the Master Base once. No framework names; instructs Lovable never to put mechanism-explaining copy on the page.

Build a single-page, mobile-first church website homepage as a reusable base I will re-skin for many different churches. Use clean, modern, production-grade design. No generic stock photos — use placeholder image slots I can swap for the church's real photos. Use CSS variables / design tokens for all colors and fonts so I can re-theme the whole page by changing a few tokens.

DESIGN TOKENS (make these easy to change in one place):
- --color-primary (used ONLY for buttons and the primary action)
- --color-secondary (headings/secondary text)
- --color-bg (light neutral background)
- --font-display (headlines only), --font-body (everything else)
- logo slot, hero image slot

TYPOGRAPHY & SPACING:
- H1 ~48–64px desktop, body 16–20px; headline must clearly dominate body.
- Consistent spacing scale in multiples of 8.
- Primary color used only for the main action button; never for in-card links.
- Everything scannable: short blocks, icon+line rows, no long paragraphs.

SECTIONS, in this exact order:
1. Sticky header: logo left; nav right (New Here, About, Times & Location, Give); one prominent button "Plan Your Visit". On mobile, always show logo + a single visible "Plan Your Visit" button (not hidden in the menu).
2. Hero: full-width real-photo slot; one warm headline naming the church and the welcome; one-line subhead for the mission; one prominent "Plan Your Visit" button; service time + city visible here or just below.
3. Times & Location band: service day/time(s), full address, embedded map, and quick practical notes (parking, what to wear, how long the service runs). Big and scannable.
4. "New here? What to expect": 3–4 short warm blocks with simple icons (what a service feels like, how long, come as you are, kids/childcare, no one will single you out). End with a "Plan Your Visit" button.
5. Mission / story: short mission line + a brief real story, paired with a photo slot. Short paragraphs, generous spacing.
6. Life of the church: an open, responsive grid of real-photo slots (no carousel). Optional small spot for a real number or short testimonial.
7. Ministries/groups: scannable cards (name, one warm line).
8. Events: a few upcoming events (date, short text, learn-more).
9. Sermons: latest message featured + embed/link slot (YouTube/FB/podcast).
10. Give: clean, trustworthy giving section with an embed/link slot and plain language.
11. Final "Plan Your Visit" band: short form (name, email, "when are you thinking of coming?") plus service time, address, map, phone, socials. Page ends on the action.
12. Footer: logo, contact, socials, copyright.

COPY RULES (critical):
- Warm, plain, human voice written to a nervous first-time visitor.
- Lead every section with the benefit to the visitor, not a label.
- Never put any sentence on the page that explains why a section exists or how it's designed to work. No marketing/strategy language anywhere visitor-facing.
- Use clearly-marked placeholder copy ([CHURCH NAME], [MISSION LINE], [SERVICE TIME], [ADDRESS], etc.) so I can swap per church.

The single most important goal: a nervous first-time visitor understands within ~5 seconds who this is, that they'd be welcome, and how to take the next step — and the "Plan Your Visit" action is reachable from the top, the middle, and the end of the page.


After scaffolding: save as your base project. Every new church = duplicate the base, change tokens + placeholder copy + image slots + one signature touch. Do not rebuild structure.

Part 7 — Per-church swap workflow (new church → live mockup)

Target: a few hours, mostly content gathering, not design.

Gather (20–30 min). From their site + Facebook + intake: logo, palette, 4–6 real photos, mission line, times, address, ministry names, sermon link, giving link. Screenshot their current homepage for the before/after.

Duplicate the base.

Set brand tokens. Colors, fonts, logo — re-skins instantly.

Swap copy. Real mission, times, address, ministry names, in their voice.

Swap imagery. Their real photos into hero + grid.

Add the one signature touch.

Fix the obvious gap. Whatever their current site most fails — usually a buried/missing Plan Your Visit — make that the visible win. That's your call ammo.

Publish to a preview URL. Send/show as the free homepage mockup.

Each pass makes the next faster. Solve something well for one church, fold it back into the base. The base appreciates; build time drops.
